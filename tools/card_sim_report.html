Read the evidence carefully

  • These are deterministic heuristic bots, not human players or an optimal solver. Random play is a stress test; tactical play is a public-information heuristic.
  • Policies receive the visible boards, their own hand, costs and the announced lane. They do not receive future draw order, enemy reserve identities or the hidden incoming card.
  • The opening hand and opponent deployment sequence are fixed in the default encounters. Seeds vary the remaining player deck and policy randomness, so opening fairness needs separate experiments.
  • Wilson intervals describe resolved seed outcomes under a particular policy. They do not measure uncertainty about real players. A zero-width bootstrap from identical outcomes does not prove that a change has no effect.
  • Surviving creatures are right-censored. Dead-creature lifespan and hit counts cannot by themselves establish average durability of all cards.
  • Scenario comparisons support conclusions about the intervention under these bots. Per-card usage is observational; frequent use or frequent wins after play does not prove that a card causes wins.
  • No-legal-play turns are turns that never offered a legal card action. Full-board turns use the board at the start of planning. Unspent Attention includes deliberate passes.
  • A turn cap is an unresolved match, not a loss. Timeouts are excluded from pacing and paired outcome changes; a high timeout rate requires investigation before trusting those summaries.
